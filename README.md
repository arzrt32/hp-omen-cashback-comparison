## The naming is doing you no favours

HP reorganised Omen in early 2025, and the result is genuinely confusing for anyone coming back to the brand. Omen Max took over the high-end slot, which pushed the ordinary Omen 16 down into budget and midrange territory. That repositioning is why reviewers spent much of the past year calling the Omen 16 a "budget gamer without the usual compromises" while also listing a mediocre display, Wi-Fi 6 only, and no USB4 or SD reader as the trade-offs.

So "hp omen" now covers three distinct ideas:

- **Omen 16** — the volume model. Cheapest way into the badge, currently built around RTX 5050/5060/5070 options.
- **Omen Max 16** — the performance model. RTX 5070 Ti up to 5090, thicker chassis, optional OLED.
- **Omen Transcend** — the portable one, in 14 and 16 inches, with Mini-LED panels and per-key RGB.

Then there's a desktop branch with the Omen 45L, and the special editions that show up every so often, like the League of Legends model that launched at Worlds with pre-orders starting at US$1,999.99.

Which one is right for you depends far less on benchmarks than on how often the machine leaves your desk.

## The corners HP cuts, and which ones actually matter

Every Omen 16 review of the past year lists roughly the same trade-offs. Whether they bother you is a personal question, so here's what each one means in practice.

**Wi-Fi 6 and no USB4 or Thunderbolt.** On the budget Omen 16 configurations, you get Wi-Fi 6 rather than Wi-Fi 7, and you won't find a Thunderbolt port or an SD card reader. If your workflow runs through a dock and an external Thunderbolt drive, this is a real limitation. If you plug in a mouse and occasionally an HDMI cable, you'll never notice.

**Single-channel memory on some configs.** Notebookcheck called this out specifically on the machine it tested. A single 16GB stick means lower memory bandwidth, and that shows up in games more than in office work. It's worth checking the configuration you're buying, and it's a strong argument for the 32GB tiers if the price gap is small.

**The display.** The most consistent criticism is the panel, not the performance. The tested Omen 16 used a 1920x1200 IPS at 144Hz with slow response times, and the reviewer's verdict was blunt: better screens on the cheaper models would have made this an easy recommendation. The higher tiers step up to 2.5K, and the Max 16 has OLED options.

**Software.** OMEN Gaming Hub is a fine tool buried in advertising. Multiple reviewers have complained about the ad load in HP's control centre. You can live with it, and there are ways to trim it, but nobody describes it as a highlight.

**Battery.** The tested Omen 16 carried a 70Wh battery, which the review called relatively small. One 2025 deal piece measured roughly 10 hours of video playback on a different config, so real-world numbers vary a lot by specification.

None of these are dealbreakers at US$849. Several of them become harder to justify at US$1,479.

## FAQ

**Does cashback change what I pay for the Omen?**
No. Your checkout total at HP is the same. Cashback is a rebate funded by the merchant's commission, so it arrives after the sale instead of reducing the price at the till.

**Can I combine cashback with an HP coupon?**
Sometimes. Cashback is not valid with vouchers or promo codes that aren't featured on ShopBack's platform. If the coupon is listed there, it stacks. If you found it elsewhere, check before applying it, because a discount that voids cashback can cost you more than it saves.

**How long until the money arrives?**
On HP, tracking typically shows within two days and the transaction confirms in around 90 days. Cashback becomes withdrawable after confirmation.

**Does it work on refurbished or Outlet Omen laptops?**
Not under the terms listed on ShopBack's HP store page. HP Outlet orders, EDU orders, and EPP orders are all excluded.

**Which Omen should I actually buy?**
If you game at 1080p or 1200p and want one machine for everything, the midrange Omen 16 is the sensible target, ideally when it drops below US$1,300. If you want a 240Hz high-resolution panel and plan to keep the machine for years, the Max 16 with an RTX 5070 Ti is the better long-term spend, and the 5090/OLED configurations run into four figures that most people don't need to reach. If the laptop travels with you, the Transcend line is the one to look at, accepting that you're paying a premium for portability.
